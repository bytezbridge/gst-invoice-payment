Why businesses are switching from Tally in 2026

Tally Prime is still a capable double-entry accounting engine, and a generation of CAs know its shortcuts by heart. But three structural shifts have made it a poor fit for many growing businesses:

1. Cloud is now the baseline. Founders, sales reps, and operations leads are mobile. Tally remains a desktop product — its TallyPrime Server and Tally on AWS options exist, but they cost ₹15,000 to ₹40,000 a year on top of the base licence and still require a Windows VM. Modern alternatives ship cloud and mobile apps as the default.

2. E-invoicing is now mandatory under ₹5 crore. Since the threshold dropped from ₹10 crore to ₹5 crore in August 2023, many SMBs need IRN generation built in. Tally supports it via a connector, but the configuration is a multi-step exercise; cloud tools handle it with a single API call.

3. Pricing has flipped. A perpetual Tally Prime Gold licence is ₹67,500 + GST plus ₹13,500 for annual TSS renewal. A modern SaaS tool with the same feature set is ₹3,000 to ₹8,000 a year. For a typical D2C brand, the three-year cost difference exceeds ₹70,000.

What to look for in a modern Tally alternative

Before comparing tools, get clear on which features actually matter to your business. The eight that we weight in this comparison are:

GST compliance: Auto-detection of CGST + SGST vs IGST based on place of supply, HSN/SAC code lookup, and one-click GSTR-1 JSON export. Anything less means you'll do it manually each month.

E-invoice (IRN) support: Direct integration with the IRP, automatic QR generation, and rollback if the IRN is rejected. Bonus: IRN cancellation within the 24-hour window from inside the app.

E-way bill: One-click generation for goods movement above ₹50,000. Pull distance from PIN-to-PIN automatically.

Multi-user and multi-device: Concurrent users with role-based access, plus a mobile app that genuinely supports invoice creation (not just viewing).

Reconciliation: GSTR-2B vs purchase register matching, bank statement import (preferably via account aggregator), and TDS report generation.

Custom fields and templates: The ability to add 4–5 custom fields to invoices (PO number, project code, sales rep) and customise the print template without writing code.

Integrations: Native connectors to your bank, payment gateway (Razorpay, Cashfree), e-commerce platform (Shopify, Amazon), and a public API for everything else.

Data ownership: Easy CSV/JSON export of every record, audit logs, and a clear contract that says you own the data — not the vendor.

Migration plan: moving off Tally in one weekend

Switching accounting software sounds intimidating, but it is a four-step exercise if you do it during a slow stretch (the first week of a month works well in India because the previous month's GSTR-1 is already filed).

Step 1 (Saturday morning) — Export from Tally Gateway → Display → List of Accounts → Export → XML/CSV Export: Customers, Suppliers, Items, Opening Balances, Tax ledgers Step 2 (Saturday afternoon) — Import into the new tool Map fields: GSTIN, place of supply, HSN, opening balance Reconcile: closing balance from Tally = opening balance in new tool Step 3 (Sunday) — Run a parallel month Issue every new invoice in BOTH systems At month-end, compare GSTR-1 JSON byte-for-byte Investigate any difference greater than ₹0 Step 4 (Month 2) — Cut over File GSTR-1 from the new tool only Keep Tally read-only for 12 months as an audit trail

The "parallel month" step is non-negotiable. It catches mismatches in HSN mapping, place of supply, and rounding before they affect a filed return. Most tools provide a free trial that comfortably covers a 30-day parallel run.

Top 6 mistakes when switching from Tally

  1. Migrating mid-cycle. Always cut over on the 1st of a month. Mid-month switches make GSTR-1 reconciliation painful and increase the risk of a notice.
  2. Skipping the parallel run. The discipline of issuing every invoice in both systems for 30 days is the single best protection against bad data. Skipping it is the most common cause of a botched migration.
  3. Not exporting Tally data before cancelling TSS. Once your Tally Software Services subscription lapses, some advanced exports stop working. Take a full export — masters, vouchers, reports — and keep it offline before you cancel.
  4. Forgetting opening balances for tax ledgers. Input tax credit (ITC) carry-forward, electronic credit ledger balances, and TDS receivables must be entered as opening balances, not as fresh transactions, or your GSTR-3B will mismatch.
  5. Choosing on price alone. The cheapest tool is rarely the best fit. Map your top 5 workflows (invoice → IRN → GSTR-1 → reconcile → file) and pick the tool that does them with the fewest clicks.
  6. Not informing your CA early. A two-week heads-up gives your CA time to set up access on the new tool and verify the chart of accounts. Surprising them on the 10th of the month is asking for trouble.

Frequently asked questions

Why are Indian businesses moving away from Tally in 2026?

Tally is desktop-first, single-user by default, and costs roughly ₹22,500 + GST for Tally Prime Silver and ₹67,500 + GST for Gold. In 2026 most growing businesses want cloud access, mobile invoicing, instant e-invoice IRN generation, and team collaboration — all of which are easier on modern SaaS tools.

Is Tally still mandatory for GST filing?

No. The GSTN portal accepts JSON uploads from any compliant tool. Any GST software that produces a valid GSTR-1 JSON, GSTR-3B summary, or e-invoice JSON is acceptable. Tally is one option among many — not a legal requirement.

Which Tally alternative is cheapest for a small business?

For freelancers and businesses under ₹40 lakh turnover, gstinvoice.app and Vyapar are the most budget-friendly. gstinvoice.app starts free with paid plans from ₹199/month, while Vyapar starts at ₹2,799/year for the desktop edition.

Can I import my existing Tally data into a new tool?

Yes. Most cloud alternatives accept Tally XML or CSV exports for masters (customers, items, ledgers) and opening balances. Voucher history is usually imported as opening trial balance rather than transaction-by-transaction to keep things simple.

Do these alternatives support e-invoicing under the ₹5 crore threshold?

Yes. As of August 2023 the e-invoice mandate applies to businesses with aggregate turnover above ₹5 crore. All seven alternatives covered here can generate IRN and QR codes via the IRP (Invoice Registration Portal) APIs once your GSTIN is enabled.

Is cloud GST software safe for storing financial data?

Reputable cloud tools encrypt data in transit (TLS 1.3) and at rest (AES-256), keep regular backups, and store data in Indian regions to comply with the DPDP Act 2023. Always verify the vendor publishes a security page and supports 2-factor authentication before migrating.

Can I run Tally and a cloud tool in parallel during migration?

Yes — and it is the recommended approach. Run both for one full GST cycle (typically a month), reconcile GSTR-1 and books, and only retire Tally once two cycles match. This avoids data loss and gives your CA time to validate the new software.
